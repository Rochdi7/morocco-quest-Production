**Date:** 24 November 2026
**For:** Morocco Quest (morocco-quest.com)
**Scope:** 50 websites that sell Morocco tours directly. Most are Moroccan local operators, plus desert specialists, luxury specialists and two international operators. Marketplaces (TourRadar, Viator, GetYourGuide, Expedia) are **not** included here; they are covered in [COMPETITOR_PRICE_BENCHMARK_2026-11.md](COMPETITOR_PRICE_BENCHMARK_2026-11.md).

**In short:**
1. **Your day trips are the most expensive in the market.** Every one of the 17 competitors that publishes an Essaouira or Atlas price is cheaper.
2. **Your multi-day tours are priced as "upper-mid / entry luxury".** They are above most Moroccan local operators, but less than half what luxury specialists charge.
3. **Your best-selling category is missing.** The 3-day Marrakech → Merzouga desert tour is the most common product in the market, on 32 of the 50 sites. Morocco Quest only offers a 5-day Sahara version.
4. **Competitors make their prices look cheaper than yours.** Many show a headline "from" price for groups of 14–17 people (e.g. "from €95", "from €179"), while the real price for 2 travellers is 2–4 times higher. Morocco Quest shows the 2-adult price, so it looks expensive next to them.

> **This changes one point from the previous report.** Against **TourRadar marketplace listings**, your Sahara 5-day ($786) and Royal Cities 6-day ($900) looked 27–29% cheap. Against **50 direct operators** they are at the market median ($157 and $150 per day vs a $137 median). Marketplace prices include a 20–30% commission, so they run higher. The updated advice is a **moderate** raise for these two tours (section 5), not the bigger raise suggested earlier.

## 4. How competitors display prices (and why it matters)

| Price display style | Sites | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Single "from" price | 20 | "From €200 per person": usually the cheapest room or the largest group |
| **Group-size price table** | **16** | 1 pax €605 · 2 pax €341 · 3 pax €260 · … · 14–17 pax €175 |
| Quote only (no public price) | 5 | "Request a quote" |
| Hotel-tier pricing | 2 | Mid-range €1,150 · Luxury €1,450 · Superior €1,850 |
| Quote with indicative ranges | 2 | "10 days ≈ $3,400–$4,900 per person" |
| Per vehicle | 1 | "From 2,400 MAD per vehicle" |
| Other (from price plus extras) | 4 | Per-tier from price, group trips, "on request" plus packages |

**Lessons for Morocco Quest:**
1. **Headline-price effect.** 5 of the best-known local sites (Marrakech Desert Trips, Saharies, Where To Go Morocco, Morocco Desert Trips, Morocco Fun Tours) put their **large-group price** in the headline and title tag, e.g. "From €95" or "From €179". The 2-person price, comparable to yours, is only in the table. In Google results and on comparison pages, your 2-adult "From $X" looks 2–3 times more expensive than it really is.
2. **Currency.** **28 of the 43** sites that publish prices quote in **EUR**, and 15 in USD. Showing EUR as well (or a currency switcher) would match what travellers see on most competitor sites.
3. **Quote-only is a minority strategy** (5 of 50). Showing prices, as you now do, is the market norm.

### 5.2 Catalogue

1. **Add a 3-day Marrakech → Merzouga desert tour.** It is the most common product in the market (32 of 50 sites).
   - Suggested price: **$420–$480 per person for 2**, the upper half of the market, matching your Madu luxury-camp positioning.
   - Also add a **2-day Zagora / Agafay** option for travellers with less time.
2. **Add a "from Fes" version** of the desert tour. Navigate Morocco, Days in Morocco and Gate Travel Morocco all sell both directions.

### 5.3 How prices are shown on the site

1. **Add a group-size price table** to every tour page (1 / 2 / 3–4 / 5–6 / 7+ travellers), like 16 of the 50 competitors.
2. **Show two "from" prices** on cards: "From $X per person for 2 · From $Y for groups of 6+". This removes the headline disadvantage without hiding the real 2-person price.
3. **Add EUR display** (a currency switcher, or EUR next to USD).
4. **Choose one positioning and match the titles to it.** Either:
   - (a) call the current tours **"boutique / premium private"** (they're priced right for that), or
   - (b) add a **"Luxury +" hotel tier** (5-star riads, priced at about $350–$450 per day) so the "Luxury" titles are backed by a luxury product.

## 8. Method and limits

1. **Discovery.** Ten web searches for Morocco tour companies (desert tours, day trips, imperial-city tours, luxury tours, Tangier/north, operator rankings), with marketplaces excluded. 64 candidate sites were found.
2. **Collection.**
   - Each site's sitemap and homepage were read.
   - The page for each reference product was picked by URL. Blog/guide pages and shared tours were excluded where a private version existed.
   - The price was read from the page, including price-per-group-size tables.
   - 20 ambiguous pages were checked by hand.
3. **Selection.** 11 sites were dropped: blocked to automated access, no product prices, or not comparable (e.g. trekking holidays). The 50 remaining are listed in section 6.
4. **Limits:**
   - These are published prices on 24 Nov 2026. Seasonal surcharges (Christmas/New Year, Easter) and final checkout prices may differ.
   - Seven sites show no public price for the desert tour, and some sites load prices with JavaScript. Those cells are marked "—" or "on request".
   - Inclusions vary (camp level, meals, guide vs driver), so the medians compare like-for-like **formats**, not identical services.
   - For "/vehicle" prices, the per-person figure used in the medians assumes 2 travellers.

**Next step:** re-run before the high season (March 2027). The scripts used are kept so the benchmark can be repeated quickly.
